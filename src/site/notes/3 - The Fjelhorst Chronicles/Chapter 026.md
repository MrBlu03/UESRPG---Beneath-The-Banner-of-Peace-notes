## Chapter 26 – The Smoke Beneath the Snow

**Middas, 10th of Evening Star, 2E 600 — Turdas, 11th of Evening Star, 2E 600**

The mountain tomb remained cold and still beneath the weight of the world above it.

Water dripped somewhere in the dark beyond the sleeping chambers, echoing through the ancient halls like the slow counting of unseen hours. The dead king slumbered in silence beneath his cairn, while the living who had trespassed into his hall spoke in hushed voices beside dim candlelight.

It was well past midday when word arrived for Kirya.

The messenger had not come alone.

J’zargo waited beyond the ruin with horses already prepared, the snow gathering upon his cloak while he watched the mountain paths below. The urgency in his eyes told the tale before his words ever did.

There had been news of Luna.

Fresh news.

Not rumor. Not hearsay passed between caravans or taverns. A true lead.

For several moments Kirya said nothing at all.

The Fangs had seen her composed beneath danger, measured beneath fear, yet the stillness that overtook her then carried something different. Beneath all the conspiracies of Kjallgrad, beneath the Daedric whispers and buried kings, there had always remained a quieter purpose guiding her steps northward.

Her sister.

The sole reason she had ever left Elsweyr.

The Vestige sensed the tension before any words were spoken. The ruin itself seemed to grow heavier as Kirya gathered the others together within the central chamber.

She explained the news plainly. There was little hesitation in her voice by the end.

This was the first true lead she had received in years. Whatever bonds had formed between herself and the Fangs of Fjelhorst, she could not allow the trail to fade cold again.

No one tried to stop her.

Periwyn lowered her gaze quietly, fingers resting atop the worn cover of her grimoire. Onasha remained still beside the fire, saying little, though the sharpness she carried so often seemed absent for a time. Even Durov, who had weathered departures before, looked uneasy beneath the flickering torchlight.

Kirya left behind what she could.

Ledgers taken from the Guild. Strange coins. Notes concerning the Coiled Serpent and the Silver Road. Every thread tied to the investigation she carefully placed into the hands of those remaining behind.

At last she approached the Vestige himself.

From her satchel she withdrew the black feather she had once carried — the crow’s token that had guided them through hidden roads and silent warnings alike.

The Vestige regarded it briefly before closing her hand back around it.

_“Keep it,”_ he told her. _“The roads of Tamriel are rarely done crossing.”_

For the first time in many days, Kirya smiled faintly.

Then she departed the tomb.

Snow crunched softly beneath her boots as she descended the mountain path toward the waiting Khajiit below. J’zargo greeted her warmly, though the urgency remained plain between them. Before long the horses were prepared, and together they rode eastward beneath the pale morning light.

The mountains swallowed them soon after.

And so the Fangs of Fjelhorst became fewer once more.

---

The following morning came gray and bitterly cold.

The companions woke stiff upon their bedrolls scattered throughout the ruin. Durov rose still wearing much of his armor, eating dry rations beside a crumbling pillar while frost clung faintly to the edges of his cloak.

Periwyn had somehow fallen asleep with her necromantic grimoire open across her face once again. The moment she woke and remembered where she was, she resumed reading immediately, blinking away exhaustion while muttering passages beneath her breath.

Onasha emerged later from the secluded corner she had claimed for herself deeper within the chamber. She stretched in silence, shoulders stiff from stone flooring and cold air alike, before joining the others near the fire.

Not long after, the Vestige summoned them to the larger hall.

He had returned during the night from scouting the location marked within the recovered ledger.

What he found troubled him.

The hidden facility existed exactly where the clues had suggested, though it had not been built by the cult itself. Rather, the servants of Clavicus Vile had established themselves within the remains of an old Dwemer ruin buried deep beneath the northern wilds.

Though lightly guarded, the site had been protected with powerful warding glyphs placed upon every entrance save the main approach.

None among them possessed the knowledge required to safely dispel such wards.

There was, however, another possibility.

The proper mixture of ingredients — particularly hagraven feathers and ground pearl — could create a varnish potent enough to dissolve the enchantments temporarily.

The Vestige spoke the idea reluctantly. Hagravens were uncommon in this region of Skyrim, and hunting one would not be simple.

Periwyn then admitted she had already encountered such a creature.

The room grew quiet.

The Vestige’s expression darkened further still when he learned she had attempted to bargain with it in exchange for magical knowledge, only for the creature to vanish after taking advantage of her curiosity.

He scolded her openly then.

Not cruelly, but with the weary frustration of one who had seen too many bargains end in ruin.

When Periwyn explained that the local villagers had been leaving offerings of human hearts and eyes to appease the hag, the Vestige let out a long sigh and muttered several deeply unflattering remarks regarding Bretons and their endless willingness to treat dark magic as negotiation rather than warning.

Sleep deprivation seemed to dull Periwyn’s judgment further. At first she insisted upon accompanying him back to the hagraven’s lair, partly from practicality and partly, perhaps, from lingering fascination with the creature itself.

The Vestige rejected the suggestion immediately.

Only after Durov and Onasha reminded her that she possessed both maps and the ability to draw them properly did Periwyn finally relent, marking the hag’s location upon parchment instead.

The remainder of the plan came together quickly.

Periwyn would seek a capable mage within Kjallgrad — preferably one trained in Alteration or Conjuration — while also procuring pearls from the alchemist. The Vestige would investigate the hagraven himself.

Their discussion was interrupted by the arrival of a crow.

Squire Peck descended noisily onto the stone table carrying several scraps of parchment bound beneath one wing. The bird had apparently spent the previous day pecking through the streets of Kjallgrad in search of rumors surrounding the Fangs.

One message concerned Durov.

A guard captain was searching for him regarding some urgent task.

The second message belonged to Onasha alone.

Vermin wished to speak with her.

Hidden places. Filthy places.

Onasha said nothing of it aloud, though she understood immediately who had sent the summons.

And so the Fangs departed the ruin once more.

---

Durov left first.

No map guided him.

No compass marked his road.

He simply chose a direction and began walking.

At the first ridge he turned right. Some time later he turned left for reasons known only to himself. At another crossing he doubled back entirely before continuing onward once more through the snow-laden wilderness.

To any observer his path would have seemed hopelessly senseless.

Yet somehow he never truly lost his way.

Perhaps some buried instinct from forgotten years still lingered within him. Or perhaps the memory of warm hearthfires and Orgnar’s sweet pork at Frostgate Hall pulled him onward more faithfully than any road-marker ever could.

Whatever force guided him, it eventually delivered him exactly where he needed to be.

The western gate of Kjallgrad emerged from the snowfall before him near dusk.

Durov paused first at the stables to visit Rimefang. The horse stood as stoic as ever within his stall, snorting softly as Durov checked his feed and patted his neck.

Only then did he continue into the city proper.

Kjallgrad looked worse than before.

Several market stalls now stood abandoned entirely, their canvas roofs sagging beneath snow and frost. Others remained shuttered despite the busy hour. The unrest surrounding the Silver Road had spread visibly through the city, hollowing the streets little by little.

Believing Captain Irlav likely waited near the guildhouse where they had first met, Durov made his way toward the Silver Road headquarters.

Two guards stopped him at the entrance, crossing their spears before the doorway.

Durov explained calmly that he had business with Vaelin Marr.

One guard disappeared inside to confirm the claim while the other remained standing stiff as carved stone. Eventually Durov was admitted within.

Vaelin himself greeted him briefly.

Captain Irlav had indeed been searching for him and would likely return before long. Until then, Vaelin encouraged Durov to make himself comfortable before retreating once more into his office.

Durov, already somewhat familiar with the guildhouse, helped himself to mead from a nearby cabinet and carried several mugs back outside.

One guard accepted awkwardly, attempting to hold the mug and shield simultaneously with little success. The second refused entirely, allowing the offered drink to spill across his greaves instead.

Durov spent the next while drinking quietly amidst the snowfall, watching the white paths winding between snow-covered pines surrounding the guildhouse.

At last Captain Irlav arrived from the city streets.

Their greeting carried genuine warmth.

Soon after, the two began walking together through Kjallgrad while Irlav explained the reason for seeking him out.

This task, unlike the others, was personal.

Not fully sanctioned by the Guild. Perhaps not even fully known to Vaelin Marr himself.

Irlav trusted Durov because he cared for the people suffering beneath the city’s unrest, and that trust had led him toward a dangerous lead concerning the disappearances plaguing Kjallgrad.

Before they could pursue it, however, another matter demanded attention.

The protest in the market had grown massive.

---

At nearly the same hour, Periwyn and Onasha descended from the mountain paths as well.

Unlike Durov, however, they spent several minutes quietly observing whether the Imperial somehow managed to travel in the correct direction unaided.

To their surprise, each time Periwyn cast Clairvoyance, the spell confirmed he remained upon the proper road.

The two women first visited the library.

Dust drifted through shafts of pale light between towering shelves while scribes and scholars muttered quietly amongst themselves. The chief librarian, Anise, quickly shushed them when their conversation rose above an acceptable volume.

When asked about skilled mages recently visiting the library, she pointed them upstairs toward a curly-haired Breton browsing the upper stacks.

Several scholars occupied the aisles above, though most ignored them entirely.

An Argonian mage dismissed Onasha curtly when she attempted to question him, insisting upon silence while he studied.

Periwyn eventually located the Breton among the Restoration texts.

The man proved to be a Priest of Stendarr.

Though not an expert in Alteration magic specifically, he possessed experience dealing with wards and agreed to aid them after learning of Periwyn’s affiliation with the Knights of the Circle.

They arranged to meet him the following day at Frostgate Hall.

From there they continued toward the alchemist’s shop.

Granny Mummu greeted Onasha warmly the moment she entered. The old woman stood happily grinding ingredients beside her apprentices while jars and herbs crowded every shelf around them.

Their familiarity resembled that of grandmother and granddaughter more than merchant and customer.

While Granny searched her stores for pearls, Onasha quietly placed a fresh red mountain flower into one of the empty vases nearby.

They exchanged gentle pleasantries before departing once more into the streets.

It was then that Onasha led Periwyn somewhere far less pleasant.

Through winding alleys and half-forgotten corners of the district they descended into the sewers beneath Kjallgrad, navigating damp corridors thick with rot and stagnant water until they reached a hidden chamber marked by a vertical rhombus enclosing a circle.

Inside gathered the city’s forgotten people.

Vagrants. Beggars. Outlaws. Runaways.

Even several workers rescued from the warehouse raid had found refuge there.

A stern woman named Tarja sat amidst stacks of parchment near the center of the room. She greeted Onasha with familiarity, though her eyes narrowed immediately upon seeing Periwyn.

The mention of the Knights of the Circle earned little trust.

Still, Periwyn offered what aid she could, even volunteering her healing magic for the sick and wounded among them. Tarja thanked her, though she explained their greater need lay elsewhere.

Rockjoint had spread through the wharfs.

The Guild’s tariffs upon medicine and ointments had left the poor with little treatment to spare, and many children had already fallen ill.

Yet Tarja sought more than healers.

Her people intended to strike directly against the Silver Road.

A protest in the market had drawn guards away from the warehouse district, leaving several guild storehouses vulnerable. While her people infiltrated the district, Onasha and Periwyn were tasked with rigging the warehouses using oil jars, ropes, and simple traps provided by the vermin.

Additional letters needed delivering as well.

Those bearing the same symbol as the sewer entrance would identify themselves and provide deathbell flowers to mark safe paths through the coming fires.

When preparations were complete, the pair departed through another sewer exit leading directly into the warehouse quarter.

The first warehouse proved difficult to enter.

The windows sat too high to reach, leaving only the guarded front entrance available. While Periwyn studied the building, Onasha distracted the weary guard posted outside, pointing repeatedly toward imaginary movement elsewhere in the district until his attention wandered just long enough.

Periwyn struck him from behind with her warhammer.

The blow killed him instantly.

Neither woman had intended it, though they still arranged the corpse carefully against nearby crates to resemble a sleeping watchman before entering the warehouse itself.

Inside they discovered little of note.

With assistance from one of Tarja’s vermin, they concealed oil jars beneath the staircase leading toward the foreman’s office and departed shortly afterward.

The second warehouse stood several streets away between worker housing and old tanneries.

This one lacked any visible guards entirely.

Only a splash of blood staining the snow hinted at where one should have stood.

Inside, another vermin already prepared the building for destruction. Oil lined the supports. Crates had been rigged carefully to spread the fire as quickly as possible.

The man handed Onasha a lit bundle of kindling.

She loaded it calmly into her sling.

Then fired.

The warehouse erupted almost instantly.

Flames burst through windows and shattered beams while black smoke rolled violently upward into the winter sky. Moments later another plume appeared farther across the district.

The first warehouse had ignited as well.

---

Elsewhere in the city, Durov stood amidst the growing protest in the market square.

Hundreds had gathered by then despite the snowfall.

Workers. Merchants. Laborers. Families.

The anger within Kjallgrad had finally become too large to remain hidden.

As liaison for matters concerning the disappearances, Durov spent much of the afternoon taking statements while Captain Irlav moved through the crowds nearby.

A trinket merchant named Harne spoke bitterly of tariffs forcing honest traders into ruin. An employee of the Eastern Tannery described workers skipping meals while the Guild increasingly forced payment through newly minted brimstone gold.

Another woman approached last.

Well dressed. Upper class.

Durov distrusted her immediately.

Yet grief stripped away much of her refinement when she spoke of her missing husband: the silk merchant Jean-Pierre LaRouche, absent for nearly a week after attending the Jarl’s gathering.

She feared something terrible had happened to him.

When the statements concluded, Irlav beckoned Durov onward once more.

Together they crossed into the older districts of Kjallgrad until they reached a place painfully familiar to the Fangs.

The abandoned Black-Foot guildhouse.

There, hidden within the shadows of a nearby alley, waited one of Irlav’s secret contacts — a figure claiming to possess new information regarding the disappearances.

But before a single word could be exchanged, the city shook.

A thunderous explosion rolled across Kjallgrad.

Durov and Irlav turned instinctively toward the distant warehouse district.

Two towering plumes of black smoke climbed into the pale evening sky.
