## Chapter 12: Embers in the Dark

**18th of Frostfall, 2E 600**

Morning broke cold over the Rift, mist clinging to the streets outside Ysmir’s hall. Onasha rose first, taking vegetables to a corner, still weary from the unrest with House Dres. Periwyn followed, chewing meat as she leafed through a spell tome. Kirya lingered by the fire, shivering against the chill. Durov sat apart with his portion of meat, silence heavy in the hall.

Adda knelt in her usual place. Onasha approached, asking of woads. Adda explained their markings—meant for the face, but sometimes stretched across the body. She promised to teach Onasha when time allowed. Kirya asked after a lute, and Adda spoke of Skyrim’s warrior-poets and the skalds who would help her find one.

By midmorning, two guards arrived, summoning them to the war room. The wind was sharp, and Kirya muttered at the cold. Onasha asked after her, but pressed no further. In the street, Eirik the Quick darted past once again, vaulting over Onasha’s latest failed trip.

Within the war room, Queen Freidr and Archmagus Mortgren awaited. The queen spoke of Jorunn the Skald-King, who saw shadows of the Grey Host in current threats. He had sent word west to Morthal, to an old ally. The Mages Guild, meanwhile, would soon arrive at Greenwall to study the strange ritual orb. Periwyn recognized the name Gabrielle Benele, though the memory eluded her.

Mortgren then gave his findings. The vampire head he had studied once belonged to Thrysnr the Unsullied, of Volkihar blood—but sired by Majorn the Ancient, thought dead for twenty years. Majorn’s name still carried weight, and Mortgren warned them to speak it rarely. He would remain in Riften for a time, sifting records and reinforcing wards. Periwyn lingered, intent on speaking with him.

Queen Freidr then told them of a fire in the Ratway. Many blamed the guards, though she suspected the Coiled Serpent. She asked the party to uncover the truth, and rebuild trust with the city’s poorest. Kirya suggested bringing food; Freidr agreed, sending them to the kitchens.

The cook, Brann, loaded a basket of bread, cheese, and ham. Durov carried it as they set out.

At the market, they asked questions. Guards recalled smoke rising from the manholes south of the stalls, late afternoon. A cabbage-seller at first scoffed, but laughed at Durov’s strange cabbage-phrased queries. Amused, he directed them to Toren in the Outlaw’s Refuge. Durov and Periwyn bought cabbages as thanks, Durov paying five gold for one.

They descended into the Ratway, arriving at the Ragged Flagon, a tavern of smoke and shadow. The barkeep confirmed the fire began deeper in the warrens. He pointed to a donation box, where Durov placed the food.

A drunk named Yannick loudly blamed the guards, faltering when pressed by Onasha. A whistle from a dark-cornered patron silenced him, the man then pointing them to Toren.

Toren, a fence, asked gold for his words. Durov haggled him down to thirty. The masked arsonists had worn strange leathers, set the fire near catacombs, and slipped away. Durov then struck a strange deal, giving Toren five hundred gold for a future moonsugar venture. Periwyn, meanwhile, tried to sell her old necromancy tome for fifteen hundred. The offer came back at thirty, but with Onasha’s help she walked away with seventy, pocketing twenty and putting fifty in the shared coffer.

The cornered patron demanded a blood contract for his truth. Durov signed, owing a future favor. The man then spoke: the arsonists’ leathers bore runes, possibly Daedric. The blaze was fed by more than oil. A serpent mark blackened one archway. Kirya, curious about the sudden growth of the Ragged Flagon, asked the masked patron what caused such a sudden stir. He explained that many of them came from Windhelm, where outlaw activity had been disrupted by bigger fish. 

Periwyn guessed the fire a distraction, meant to enter the Hall of the Dead. The group rushed there. Sigrid, keeper of the dead, tore through her shelves in panic—her autopsy notes on the fallen Dunmer were gone. There had been no forced entry. The suspicion became certainty.

They returned to Freidr, laying out what they had found. She reimbursed Durov for the gold he had spent and granted them wide authority to pursue the Coiled Serpent. Papers would follow in days, but for now the hunt was theirs.

The party retired to Ysmir’s hall as the night deepened, the scent of rain and smoke still clinging to their cloaks.
