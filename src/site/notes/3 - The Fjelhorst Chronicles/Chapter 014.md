## Chapter 14: The Frozen Hunt

**3rd of Sun’s Dusk, 2E 600**

The Fangs awoke to pale light cutting weakly through the shutters, the morning chill slipping between the gaps. Periwyn rose first, checking her weapons and spending quiet moments with Ashen, running a brush along her mane. Onasha and Kirya followed suit, tending to Mossback and Sunsnow, while Durov’s absence hung like a faint question none voiced aloud.

Seeking word of the frozen hunters, they made their way to the market. Onasha sold a rack of antlers she’d taken from the woods days before, adding 95 gold to the communal coffer.

Questioning a local hunter yielded their first lead. The man’s brother had been among the frozen dead. Still raw with grief, he offered little but pointed them toward the Jarl, who was said to be handling the matter.

At the Jarl’s Longhouse, they were received not by Jarl Gorm but by his Huscarl, Helga Cairn-Shield. She was surprised to learn the Fangs were outsiders, but her tone softened when she heard Queen Freidr’s name. Gorm, she explained, had taken ill with bone-rot — what the locals called Rockjoint. The people were wary, their memories of Greenwall still bleeding fresh. She urged them to keep their work quiet.

Helga also revealed that one hunter had survived the encounter — a man named Nilan. He had come to report what happened, but vanished soon after. Guards sent to his home found nothing. Handing Onasha a key, Helga suggested the Fangs might fare better than her men, and pointed northeast toward the site of the incident.

---

Nilan’s house sat half-buried in frost, its chimney long cold. Onasha and Kirya circled the outside, spotting a spoiled meal through the window. Periwyn, focusing as Mortgren had taught her, felt the faint pulse of death lingering near the back wall.

The key turned stiffly in the lock. Inside, the air was sour — spoiled stew on the table, dust settling over the room. Trophies lined the walls: wolves, bears, saber cats, the relics of a long hunt. Yet Kirya’s eyes caught a detail — a bow hung wrong near the door, not the hand of a seasoned hunter.

Periwyn followed the pull of her sense to the bedroom. Beneath a rug, she found a hatch. The hinges groaned as she lifted it, releasing a wave of rot. A flickering candle burned below.

They descended into a cramped cellar, where a man lay bound against the wall — bruised, bloodied, long dead. The sharp scent of Nightshade filled the air. Onasha recognized it at once: the same poison that had killed Adrith Velan months before. On a nearby table sat two vials and a mortar stained black. One vial held liquid as dark as pitch.

Periwyn examined the corpse. Sunken eyes. Bite marks at the neck. He had been turned — a thrall to a vampire. Her stomach turned, but she steadied herself. Onasha gathered the vials and a fallen Nightshade petal before they left for the woods.

---

The forest north of Faldarstad lay in uneasy silence. No birds, no rustle — only the occasional crack of branches beneath unseen weight. They passed a shred of hide tangled high in a tree, and the carcass of a deer, skinned and abandoned. The woods were watching.

In a shallow clearing they found two hunters frozen mid-motion — one with his sword drawn, the other with a bow half-pulled. Frost had claimed them long ago. Around them lay the signs of battle: splintered boughs, deep gashes carved into bark — too broad for bear, too long for wolf.

Following the clawed path led them to the remnants of a hunting lodge, half-collapsed and heavy with dust. Blood and decay hung in the air — and within, a vampire waited.

He did not attack. He leaned against the wall, clutching a wound that ran deep across his side, carved by whatever beast had left those marks. His voice carried arrogance rather than fear. He boasted of serving Majorn’s Coven — a survivor of Greenwall, sent to test mortal strength. Greenwall, he said, was only a field experiment, a taste of what was to come. He had turned Nilan into a thrall merely to feed while he healed.

When Onasha mentioned Nilan’s fate — and the Vestige — his grin faltered. Yet his tone soon hardened again. “You’re too late,” he sneered. “Fear spreads faster than fire. Soon all of them will break.”

The Fangs shared a glance. There was nothing more to learn. Onasha drew first, Kirya followed, and Periwyn’s lightning struck last. His dying words echoed faintly: “My work here is done.”

---

On the road back, Onasha questioned Periwyn’s hesitation. Periwyn spoke of her coma, of the dreams — or visions — she’d had while trapped between life and death. Onasha dismissed it as fevered imagining. But when Periwyn showed her the feather given by the stranger in black, the forest seemed to listen.

Crows watched from the treeline. One hopped closer and spoke — not in voice, but thought. It confirmed what Periwyn had said. Its master, the Crow Father, dealt in omens and feathers, leaving tokens for those he marked.

Overwhelmed, Onasha tried to return the cryptic scroll one of the birds had once given her, saying they had enough burdens already. The crow tilted its head and replied, “You will understand in time.”

Then came a howl — low, pained, unnatural — rolling from the deeper woods. They took it as their cue to leave. At the forest’s edge, pale blue eyes gleamed between the trees, watching until the road swallowed them.

---

They returned to Nilan’s home, building a small pyre behind the house. As the flames took him, Onasha and Kirya relieved the walls of a few trophies still worth selling.

At the longhouse, they reported to Helga, advising her to keep the townsfolk’s spirits high, to remind them of the Nords’ strength in the face of fear. Her expression softened, her spine straightened — their words struck true. She agreed to let them occupy Nilan’s house for the time being, pending the Jarl’s approval.

That night, they made the house their own. Kirya and Onasha gathered wood for the hearth, while Periwyn descended once more into the cellar. There, by the lone candlelight, she opened Mortgren’s book and began her next lesson.

She whispered the names of the dead — her mother, her father, her brother, her mentor — and those met briefly along the way. Voices whispered back. She held steady, as taught, until they faded into silence. Then, with trembling hands, she wrote what she felt, not what she wished to feel, and left the [[Periwyn Lore/Periwyn Items/Exercise 3 - The Letter\|note]] upon the table.

Upstairs, the others settled near the fire, their bedrolls laid in a rough circle. Outside, the wind rose, carrying faint echoes of the howl they had heard before.
