## Chapter 31 – It Never Is

**Fredas, 12th of Evening Star, 2E 600 (night)**

While Periwyn and Onasha ducked their heads into the walkway, the Vestige drew Durov aside for one last briefing.

The entrances they had found, he explained, likely connected to ventilation tunnels snaking throughout the facility — passages the team could follow, but not easily, and certainly not quickly, to the final chamber where the ritual was almost certainly underway. That navigation rested with Durov, waiting outside. They would see to it he knew where they were going by lowering strips of red cloth from the vents, hanging them and waving them as they advanced through the lower maintenance corridors.

With that, the Vestige leapt back up onto the walkway and entered behind the others.

Just then, a handful of guards emerged from within to investigate the blast.

Durov, with the luck he seemed to carry like a second weapon, managed to misdirect them entirely — sending them out into the surrounding wilderness on a wild chase after nothing. To explain his own presence after the search party moved off, he invented an excuse about his armor being filthy, and used it to walk back into the ruin unchallenged to "clean it off."

The gate, for all practical purposes, was his.

---

Inside, the infiltration team snaked their way through the vast expanses of the Dwemer ruin.

High-pressure steam pipes lined the shafts, hissing and ticking in the dark. One ruptured valve vented scalding steam across Periwyn's arm, drawing a cry of pain she swallowed before it could echo. Along various corridors, strange shoots opened in the walls — lined with sharp, movable teeth, as if they might open on their own in some mechanical reflex. The Vestige explained that these were automated defenses, designed to deploy Animunculi when triggered. The likelihood of that was lower now, he noted — the majority of the defenses would have already spent themselves upon the cult's own entry into the ruin.

Through floor grates, since windows, and various other openings, they counted over a dozen guards still inside — doing rounds, checking provisions, many visibly on edge from the sound of the blast. Along the way, whenever the team spotted Durov through the vents, they lowered a strip of red cloth and waved it at him — signaling that they were following the intended path, and advancing through the lower maintenance corridors as planned.

---

Eventually, after a great many turns and more than a few dead ends, the vents delivered them to an elevated viewpoint overlooking the central chamber.

It was a large, pillared Dwemer hall, lined with brass piping and drainage grates, all converging upon a glowing sacrificial circle at its heart. What struck them first were the captives.

Kjallgrad's missing citizens — all tied up, all unconscious, laid out across the chamber floor like offerings waiting to be collected. All of them, clearly, slated for sacrifice to Clavicus Vile.

Among them, Onasha recognized a familiar face. A merchant she had bought silk from, weeks ago, who had gone missing not long after.

His Illustrious Magnificence Jean-Pierre LaRouche, Marquis of the Sapphire Coast, Baron of the Nine Looms, Keeper of the Royal Cocoon Seals, and Patriarch of House LaRouche.

Presiding over the altar, flanked by two imposing guards, stood a creature out of nightmare. A form of melting off it like wax down a candle, revealing towering horns upon an even more towering frame.

The Vestige's warning came low and immediate: a Xivilai.

A monster without a true master. A Dremora so aggressive that the only sensible way to fight one was to stay out of its range — and Periwyn and Durov would soon understand why. Despite all his own expertise in killing such beasts, the Vestige could not join them. When the fight began — and it would begin — the cultists would come rushing in like a flood, and he had better odds holding the flood back alone than the Fangs did fighting it together.

And so it was decided.

The Vestige leapt down out of the shafts and went to deal with the cultists.

The Fangs would fight what looked like a nightmare on legs.

Durov, as if to demonstrate his charm and quick wits, had a master plan.

One he elected not to share with his allies in the vents above him.

---

Durov simply opened the door.

As if he owned the place.

He stumbled inside, all feigned confusion, and when the Xivilai and its guards spotted him they told him — not gently — that the area was off limits to a lowly sentry such as himself. Even his protests of connection to Lord Marr did not move them. So Durov spun a story instead: he had gotten lost, he said, while looking for a drink.

He looked the Xivilai up and down — a creature holding an axe so large it might as well have been a person — and challenged it to a drinking contest.

The creature, bemused, asked whether he did not mean to challenge one of his guards.

Durov did not.

Perhaps out of sheer bafflement at this bold sentry, the Xivilai agreed, and they established ground rules. Each half-litre flagon had to be slammed, drained empty, and then flipped over to show no liquid left behind. Only then would another be poured.

And so it began.

Each contestant offered toasts as they drank — to useful lackeys, to prosperous futures, and even, at one point, to questions answered. It was during one of these toasts that the Xivilai noticed the crest upon Durov's armor: a two-headed eagle. Durov claimed it a simple family crest, nothing more. But the creature said it looked familiar — from a battle it had fought long ago, in the city of Bruma.

Durov brushed the remark aside, and kept matching the nightmare drink for drink, holding its full attention while, in the vents above, the others crept into better and better positions.

Eventually — perhaps the twelfth or thirteenth flagon in — the Xivilai grew bored.

And drew its axe.

For how could a simple sentry, one without the clearance to stand in this chamber at all, be permitted to leave alive after all that he had seen?

And just then — right then — Onasha and Periwyn sprung the ambush.

---

Quick bombs landed near the guards, paralyzing them both where they stood.

The Xivilai, stunned by the suddenness of it, could only stare at Durov — whose shield and sword had already come around with swift precision. Periwyn leapt down from the vent and executed the first paralyzed guard with a single slice through the neck, beheading him cleanly. While the Xivilai stood frozen, Durov leapt at the second guard, plunging his sword through the creature's chest.

The Xivilai's rage, when it finally broke free, was absolute.

It cleaved through them with its great axe. Durov blocked — and learned in one blow how badly he had underestimated the creature's strength. The hit landed as though his shield were not there at all, and he was glad, retrospectively, that it had been. Periwyn was not so lucky. The axe, after striking Durov, simply kept going — and struck her as well. The pain was unbearable. Had she stood but a few centimeters closer, it would have cleaved her in half.

She leapt back out of the creature's reach and retaliated with two searing beams of light, burning the Xivilai across its face.

Every blow they struck seemed futile. The creature simply kept standing, barely looking hurt.

But then came the moment that changed everything.

Durov, in a desperate attempt to pierce the Xivilai with his sword, managed instead to loop his blade around its arm — and disarm it, throwing the great axe behind him. Periwyn seized the opening immediately, sprinting for the weapon and kicking it away, denying the Xivilai any chance to reclaim it.

The creature was undeterred.

It charged straight for Durov, and — despite arrows, spells, and swords striking it with fury — eventually managed to seize him like a wet rag and pin him to the ground.

But in its arrogance, it had put itself in a bind.

As its hands moved toward Durov's throat, Periwyn's lightning seared across its flesh — and in that instant of pain, Durov resisted, twisted, and tore himself free.

The Xivilai tried to do the same, but just as it looked up, an arrow from one of the vents above went straight into its throat.

And as it gargled on its own blood, Durov shoved his hand into the wound, gripped hard, and ripped the head and upper spine free from the body.

---

With the Xivilai dead, the Fangs moved to help the captives.

Onasha used ingredients from her pouch to wake the silk merchant — and upon waking, a tearful, disoriented LaRouche began reciting his many titles before she could gently calm him. Durov knelt beside them, handed him a map leading back to Kjallgrad, and advised him, quietly, to be cautious of the civil unrest inside the city walls.

The Fangs untied the remaining captives, ensured each of them could walk, and made their way back through the ruin.

There, upon opening the heavy doors into the first great room before the exit, their eyes saw — and their noses smelled — the horror of what remained of the facility.

Leaning against one of the pillars stood the Vestige.

Bloodied. Exhausted. Surrounded by well over two dozen guards — or what remained of them. Blood pooled across the chamber floor. The corpses lay shriveled, as if all life had been siphoned out of them through some unseen hunger.

One was still moving. Barely.

Durov recognized him at once — the exhausted guard he had swapped places with at the gate.

He took a nearby dagger, cupped the guard's face gently, and plunged it into the man's chest with a final, quiet words: _"It was nothing personal."_

With his final breath, the guard replied: _"It never is."_

Then, as quickly as they had come, they left — carrying the unconscious priest back with them to the old tomb.

---

The march back was quiet, but the tension upon it was thicker than it had ever been.

A priest. An innocent man. Nearly dead, due to their negligence.

Due to Periwyn's negligence, as the Vestige put it.

Back in the tomb, he laid the priest upon a table and examined him. Death, he said at last, might have been more merciful. The ward's magic, as far as the Vestige could tell, had locked the priest inside his own mind — trapped in a void he could not escape. Not without expert help.

_"Expert,"_ he said — emphasizing the word, letting it hang, looking directly at Periwyn as he did.

Mirroring, almost word for word, what he had told them only days before.

There was nothing more any of them could do for him. And so the Vestige sent word to mages far more knowledgeable than himself — and now all they could do was wait.

But the word still hung in the air.

And the Crow Father thought it warranted repeating.

_Expert,_ he said.

Periwyn had mentioned hiring a mage. A mage. Not a priest. She should have known better — she *should* have known better — than to hire a Priest of Stendarr, the god of Mercy and Justice, to do the work of an expert mage.

Noticing that the blame was falling solely upon Periwyn, Onasha stepped in, trying to shift some of it to herself — they should have tested him, should have checked, should have—

But to the Vestige, none of that mattered.

Periwyn was the one who studied magic. Who had worked with priests before. Periwyn was the one who wore that insignia of the Knights of the Circle. She was the one whose judgement had lapsed. She should have known better. Even in the far, cold reaches of Skyrim, the Knights of the Circle carried immense reverence and respect. Despite her questionable heritage, that emblem meant something — and her reckless decisions would lead it to ruin and dishonor.

Periwyn could only silently accept the criticism.

Her own guilt, she knew, far exceeded any external scolding.

Racked with guilt for dragging an unprepared priest into mortal danger, she wrote an extensive letter of apology, detailing what had happened, explaining that healers were on the way, and taking full responsibility for his plight. She signed and stamped it with both her family seal and that of the Knights of the Circle, and tucked the parchment securely inside the priest's robes — so that he would find it upon waking.

Before resting, Durov placed a reassuring hand upon the priest's shoulder.

He said, quietly, that the nightmare would be over soon.
