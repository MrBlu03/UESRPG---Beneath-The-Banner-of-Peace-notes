## Chapter 22 - To a Prosperous Future

**Turdas, 27th of Sun’s Dusk, 2E 600**

Kjallgrad woke early that morning, long before the sun reached the mountain ridges. Smoke rose from chimneys, carts rolled across the bridge, and the city prepared itself for an event meant to display stability more than celebrate it.

Durov rose first. Sleep had not troubled him, though the day ahead carried a weight he could not name. After dressing, he went downstairs and ordered breakfast — sweet pork and bread — finding the innkeeper worn thin from a night spent cooking for the gathering at the Jarl’s Hall. The Guild of the Silver Road, which owned much of the city’s trade, had demanded large quantities of food. Refusal had never been an option.

His own obligation awaited him at the guildhouse.

There, Guard Captain Irlav greeted him with an ease that suggested familiarity earned elsewhere. Durov noticed the crest on Irlav’s shield mirrored his own, an observation that lingered longer than either man spoke of it. Their conversation drifted toward old campaigns and shared habits of soldiers, and Durov answered carefully, shaping his words around the gaps in his memory.

The task itself seemed simple.  
Vaelin Marr would attend the gathering, and Durov, along with Irlav and another guard, would serve as personal protection. As precaution, Irlav handed Durov a small vial and instructed him to drink it when the Jarl began speaking. He described it as harmless. Durov accepted it without argument, though the instruction stayed with him.

When morning finally broke, Durov passed through the market for provisions and encountered a familiar figure — the cabbage merchant, now selling potatoes instead. The explanation was practical: seasons changed, farms adapted. The man introduced himself as Olaf and spoke of a small farm near Kjall’s Crossing. Durov promised to visit if his travels allowed. Such promises were easy to make and rarely meaningless, even when uncertain.

He returned to the inn as the others began their day.

---

Onasha’s caution had reshaped their rooms. Windows and doors were boarded, not out of fear alone but habit. Kirya questioned it; Onasha answered simply that the city did not feel right.

Periwyn soon revealed the letter that shifted their plans.  
Guildmaster Jorven Halliksson had invited her, by name, to the gathering, allowing her to bring companions. Opportunity and suspicion arrived together. Refusing would close doors; accepting meant stepping into something arranged without their consent.

Kirya shared rumors she had heard through traders and caravan talk — that the Jarl had been behaving strangely, that some unknown object was kept within his private quarters. Rumors were rarely proof, but they often pointed toward something real.

They decided to act on the chance.

Periwyn and Onasha would attend openly. Durov would be present as a guard and keep distance. Kirya would search where guests could not. When Durov showed them the vial he had been given, Onasha and Kirya identified it as a potion of spell absorption. The precaution raised more questions than it answered. They purchased their own supply from an elderly alchemist, who mentioned that guards had recently ordered a large batch.

Preparations finished, they separated.

---

The Jarl’s Hall had been transformed for the event. Tables overflowed with food, banners hung heavy from rafters, and merchants filled the space with quiet negotiations disguised as conversation. The gathering carried the familiar tone of trade politics — polite, calculated, and watchful.

Durov remained near Vaelin Marr, observing rather than participating. Vaelin moved easily among the merchants, but his attention lingered when Periwyn entered the hall.

Speeches followed.  
Guildmaster Halliksson spoke of prosperity, cooperation, and trust, praising the Jarl and the Guild’s role in the city’s stability. The words were expected, and most received them as such.

Periwyn spent the time rebuilding connections thought lost. Merchants who had known her family offered condolences and opportunity in equal measure. Trade routes were discussed before goods existed to travel them. Reputation carried weight even in absence.

Vaelin’s approach was more deliberate. He spoke of change coming, of the difficulty of remaining neutral when politics shaped trade so directly. His offer of partnership was patient rather than urgent, but it carried an implication that waiting might have consequences.

During the exchange, Onasha noticed the pendant at his neck — the ouroboros, unmistakable. Recognition passed quietly between them. They said nothing.

---

While the hall occupied itself with appearances, Kirya moved through the sewers beneath it.

The tunnels were uneven — some recently maintained, others old enough to feel abandoned. She marked her path and searched methodically. The corpse she discovered bore little of use beyond a rusted key, yet the key proved enough to open passages that had been closed to others.

The deeper she went, the older the stone became.

Eventually she reached a wine cellar large enough to confirm her suspicion that she was beneath the Jarl’s residence. Moving upward required patience and timing. Through careful movement and the use of her tincture, she slipped past guards and entered the private chambers.

What she found suggested something had happened recently.

A bloodied fur cloak hidden within a closet. Soot clinging to the fabric. Signs of hurried concealment rather than careful storage. Near the bed, a locked box held a small obsidian stone etched with Daedric runes, warm to the touch despite its size. Kirya wrapped it, returned the box to its place, and withdrew before anyone noticed the absence.

---

Back in the hall, the Jarl stepped onto the balcony to address the gathering.

Those who had prepared drank their potions as he began. His speech echoed the same themes already spoken — unity, cooperation, and the importance of supporting the Guild. As he spoke, a wave of warmth passed through the room. For the Fangs, it faded quickly. Whether others noticed or felt its full effect remained unclear.

The gathering did not end because of the speech.

Two guards were found dead near the entrance, their deaths abrupt enough to shift the mood immediately. Explanations spread quickly: criminal groups, foreign influence, familiar names invoked to contain uncertainty. The event was cut short. Guests were escorted away with reassurances that the disruption was contained.

Durov assisted where needed before returning to the inn.

---

Kirya attempted to find the Vestige at the old guildhouse, hoping for insight into the stone, but found the place empty — cleared of signs that anyone had been there recently. Whether this was precaution or coincidence, she could not tell.

By evening, the Fangs regrouped.

Their discoveries aligned more than they expected. Vaelin’s pendant confirmed suspicion of his involvement with the Coiled Serpent. The cloak and the stone suggested activity within the Jarl’s quarters tied to Daedric influence. Periwyn recognized the runes but could not interpret them.

Without guidance, they chose caution.

Removing the steward without proof risked exposure. Acting too quickly favored those already prepared. They decided instead to wait, observe, and arrange a meeting with Vaelin under the pretense of interest in his offer. Information, for now, mattered more than confrontation.

The stone remained with them.

They retired with the understanding that the gathering had revealed more than it intended — not through speeches or agreements, but through what had been hidden beneath the Longhall and nearly overlooked.

The city returned to its usual rhythm by nightfall.  
But Kjallgrad felt different to them, as cities often do once their seams have been seen.
