## Chapter 8: The Lodge of Clan Cold-Moor
**4th of Frostfall, 2E 600**  
The morning in Riften was grey and cold. Onasha stood quietly in the corridor outside their rooms, waiting for Kirya and Periwyn. When they emerged, the air was heavy with unfinished thoughts. The conversation that followed was subdued, laced with vulnerability.

Kirya brought up what had happened—how she'd seen the two of them slip away to attempt to raise the Bone Hawk. Onasha responded first, telling Kirya not to get too attached, her voice tense. "That only gets you hurt." But the words betrayed more than she let on—a flicker of care she refused to admit.

Periwyn, arms crossed, eyes sharp, told Kirya to mind her own business. But Kirya, calm and unwavering, spoke of the sister she had lost, and how she didn't want to watch the same thing happen again. "I care about you. Both of you. I didn't think I would, but I do." Her voice cracked slightly, and for a moment, none of them said anything.

They made their way to the great hall, the silence between them settling like dust. Periwyn barely touched her food, scribbling away in her journal. Then they set out for The Sleeping Stag Inn.

The inn, nestled in a quieter part of Riften, carried the scent of burning pine. Skalds chatted by the hearth, beneath a massive elk head. In the corner sat their client: Hadvar of Clan Cold-Moor. Finely dressed, boots on a chair, wine half-drunk, lamb picked at lazily—he was out of place in every sense.

Hadvar, without much courtesy, explained his problem: his newly acquired ancestral lodge was haunted. Whispers in the dark, voices calling his name. He called it "Kahru's Hollow," drew a horrendous map, and waved them off. Periwyn, appalled by the quality of the map, produced a more accurate one from his ramblings.

Before leaving town, the party sought out more information. The two skalds—Senya and Sonya—were twins, keepers of song and story. They told the tale of Clan Cold-Moor: how Sven Cold-Moor had loved Lagertha of Clan Red-Grain. How the love was deemed shameful. Lagertha was flayed alive and thrown in the village well. In his grief, Sven walked into the barrow. That night, the entire clan was found dead.

Still curious, the party went to Ysmir's Hall, where the Cult of Ysmir was now led by a shaman named Adda. Younger than expected, her eyes were covered by a blindfold, and she wore a headdress adorned with antlers and feathers. Runes marked her arms.

Adda's words were cryptic, but insightful. The land around Kahru's Hollow, she warned, was cursed—spirits twisted by pain and rage. Yet, mercy might reach them. She spoke to each of them with eerie precision. Periwyn she called the merchant's daughter—unnerving Periwyn into silence. To Onasha, she spoke of the fox spirit, calling it a nature spirit drawn to her path. To Kirya, she offered quiet hope: "Your sister will come. When she can."

The road east was lined with signs of the past. At a farmstead near the forest's edge, a man named Olgierd warned them: his goat had vanished into the trees near the lodge. He wasn't foolish enough to follow. He said if one listens near the well, one can still hear Lagertha's voice—sometimes crying, sometimes laughing.

Deeper in the woods, they found an overgrown shrine, long forgotten. At a fork in the road, they encountered a half-toppled cairn. Periwyn noted its resemblance to Wyrd magic, while Onasha compared it to the warding stones of Black Marsh.

Then came the fog. Dense, unnatural. It wound between the trees like something alive. In it, faint whispers echoed: "She still weeps." "He loved her."

They arrived at the lodge in the early afternoon. Though the surrounding buildings lay in ruin, the lodge stood intact, untouched by time. Periwyn whispered that it felt as though the Divines themselves had turned their eyes away. In the center of the courtyard, the well remained. Durov noticed tension on the rope—and a skeleton at the end.

A pale figure appeared in the window. Onasha waved. The figure waved back, gently. Then the lodge door creaked open.

Inside, the hearth was lit. Wine sat open on the table. As they entered, the door slammed shut. The spirit revealed herself: Lagertha. She welcomed them warmly. It had been so long, she said, since she'd spoken with the living.

When asked about Arkay, she seemed confused. "I know no god of that name."

Periwyn paled. Lagertha was far older than they thought.

Their conversation was cut short by the clanging of metal. Lagertha's demeanor changed. "He doesn't like when I have guests," she warned.

Moments later, the door exploded inward. Periwyn dodged just in time. A draugr entered—Sven, in rusted armor, eyes glowing cold blue.

He demanded they leave. But the party saw the truth in him. With careful words, they offered to help. He relented. "Until morning," he said. "Then this ends."

Sven led them to the master bedroom. Behind a wardrobe, they found a chest, locked and old. Inside: a pouch of ancient coins, and a strange mask. Periwyn sketched it carefully, choosing not to disturb it for now.

As a Knight of Arkay, Periwyn suggested consecration to prevent resurrection. She lacked the ingredients. Kirya volunteered to return to Riften, taking the sketch with her to ask questions.

While she was gone, the others explored the basement. The stench was unbearable. Onasha and Periwyn retched.

There, they found a corpse with a parchment and a key. Sven recognized it: a man who had once tried to help. The parchment was an excerpt from Sven's journal.

Outside, Durov pulled Lagertha's remains from the well—except her head, which had fallen in. Onasha, able to breathe underwater, descended. She retrieved the skull, a necklace given to Lagertha by Sven, and a small lockbox.

Durov cut wood for the pyre. As the sun dipped low, Periwyn meditated near the lodge, and they waited—for Kirya, for answers, and for the morning to come.
