## Chapter 16 – Inheritance of Ash

**6th of Sun's Dusk, 2E 600**

The dawn rose thin and joyless over [[Info/Locations/Falderstad\|Falderstad]]. From the west came the hollow toll of the Hunter's Bell — a sound that rolled across frozen rooftops and empty streets, steady and solemn as mourning. The hearth in the Fangs' hall burned low, its embers barely whispering warmth into the cold stone.

[[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Onasha\|Onasha]] stirred first, groggy from the mead of the night before, and plunged her head into a bucket of icy water to banish the weight of sleep. [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Periwyn\|Periwyn]] followed suit, quiet and deliberate, while [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Kirya\|Kirya]] busied herself with tea — steam rising from her cup like faint mountain mist. [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Durov\|Durov]] was already awake, armor half-fastened, his movements sharp and uncharacteristically brisk. His eyes, clear and bright despite the hour, held a restless vigor the others had seldom seen.

Outside, the city lay still. No hunters roamed, no dogs barked; only the wind and the tolling of the bell filled the dawn. When the Fangs reached the market square, they found hides left to cure torn and raked open — a silent testament to the night's intrusion.

At the center stood [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Svala\|Svala]], her hands raw from the bell's rope. She greeted them and gathered the hunting band: [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Torik\|Torik]], a scholar of the Clever Craft and once of [[Info/Locations/Winterhold\|Winterhold]]; [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Bjorr\|Bjorr]], the broad-shouldered woodsman with his rune-carved axe; [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Ingrin\|Ingrin]], youngest of the trackers and swiftest of the lot; [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Runa\|Runa]], a veteran hunter hardened by countless winters; [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Alma\|Alma]], huntress of Kjallgrad; and, to the Fangs' unease, young [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Hoki\|Hoki]], the boy from the market days before.

The sight of Hoki stirred protest among the Fangs, for the hunt to come was no place for a child. Yet the boy's resolve held until Periwyn took him aside. Whatever passed between them, her words carried weight enough that he bowed his head and turned back toward the town. When she returned, her expression had softened, touched by something mournful.

As the others spoke with Svala, plans took form: this day's work was to learn the creature's shape and nature — to unearth its weakness before it hunted them in turn. Onasha, recalling the strange rune Durov had found, brought him to Torik. The scholar studied the carved symbol, murmuring its name — _ōþala_, the rune of heritage and inheritance — and offered his theories: the creature might seek to claim the land as its birthright, or reclaim it from those who had usurped it.

Durov described again the figure he had glimpsed in the snow: tall and sinewy, cloaked in hides like woven shrouds, its shadow followed by wolves who served it not out of loyalty but compulsion. Before they departed, Torik brushed each brow with woad paint, murmuring old invocations to sharpen their sight beneath the forest's dark boughs. Durov turned away as the scholar lifted his visor, unwilling to show the face that hid behind steel.

---

Their first hour beneath the trees passed in silence. When the sun broke the horizon, the air had already soured — too still, too hollow. Every step sounded like a drumbeat in a forgotten tomb. Tracks wound backward across the snow; totems hung from branches, fresh and dripping red.

By midmorning the forest deepened, the light thinning to a dull, amber haze. On a frostbitten trunk they found the rune again — carved deep, the bark bleeding resin. Torik observed it with quiet reverence, noting how the creature's marks seemed not merely to stain but to _bind_ the trees, as though claiming their life as its own.

Later they reached a half-frozen stream, its surface dark with the hue of old rust. Iron nails studded the surrounding trunks — warnings, or offerings once made to appease a god that did not forgive. Among them stood a totem, blackened and half-ash, as though burned by lightning. In the snow pooled a strange red sap, thick and faintly steaming. Onasha's sharp curiosity drove her to test it; its warmth and copper tang revealed it not as sap at all, but blood. The realization spread among them like heat from a spark — the creature could bleed, and thus, could be slain.

---

Periwyn lingered apart from the group, reaching into the stillness with her necromantic senses. The forest answered faintly — a pulse beneath the ground, threads of remembrance clinging to the roots. Torik's gaze met hers; in his eyes, understanding and warning in equal measure. Nothing was said aloud, yet both knew such knowledge was best kept unspoken.

Without warning, the trees parted. They entered a clearing barren of snow and sound. At its center lay a mound of fused bones — human, wolf, elk, bear — arranged in concentric rings. Around them hung hides, flayed clean and stretched taut, layered into grotesque patterns that caught the dim light like frozen waves.

Torik's breath grew thin as he recognized the sigils etched into the bones: markings of _Harcane_, the Ragged Lord, blood-kin of madness and mockery. The clearing was no mere lair. It was a shrine — a sanctum raised to the creature's own reflection.

They left in silence, for the day was dying and the forest had begun to stir.

---

Their path home twisted upon itself. Tracks vanished, landmarks dissolved into mist. When Ingrin's own voice echoed from the trees ahead, panic gripped her. The others subdued her gently, until her breathing steadied. Then a sound — a snap, a stifled cry — and Bjorr was gone. They found him soon after, hanging from a branch, his body limp and swaying like a cruel talisman.

The woods whispered laughter.

Desperation drove Torik to his knees. He poured fire salts into his brazier and whispered a prayer older than the Empire. Flame erupted skyward, brilliant and fierce. The air shuddered as though the forest itself screamed, and somewhere unseen, something vast recoiled. The scholar spat blood but rose again, eyes alight with revelation: the Ragged Lord feared the flame.

Guided by that burning light, the survivors found the forest's edge at last.

---

Night had fallen when Faldarstad's torches came into view. Relief broke into motion; they quickened their steps. Then the howl came — close, vast, too near to belong to any mortal thing.

The creature struck from the treeline. Durov turned on instinct, his shield taking the blow meant for Periwyn. The impact hurled them both into the snow. When they looked up, the Ragged Lord had already vanished into the dark.

---

Within the safety of the walls, Svala gathered the shaken hunters by the market fire. The tolling bell echoed once more, low and grim. Word spread quickly — of the hunt, of Bjorr's death, of what they had seen in the grove. Hoki, who had waited in town, met them with a silence far older than his years.

Torik, pale and trembling, gave orders through the cold: more archers on the walls, tar and oil for the arrows, braziers kept lit through the night. The Fangs would rest, then spend the 7th preparing salves, tonics, and firebombs for the coming trial. When the sun rose again, they would work by its light; and on the morning of the 8th of Sun's Dusk, they would stand once more beneath the Hunter's Bell to finish what had begun.

That night, the wind swept low through the streets, carrying the smell of pine and distant smoke. From the forest's edge came no sound, no stir — only a silence so complete it seemed to listen back.

The Ragged Lord waited, patient as frost.
