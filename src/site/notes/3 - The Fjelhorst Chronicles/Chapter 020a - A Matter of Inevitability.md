## Chapter 020a - A Matter of Inevitability

**Loredas, 22nd of Sun’s Dusk, 2E 600**

The morning found [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] awake without reluctance. Habit carried him through the familiar motions: armor secured piece by piece, straps tightened with the same care as always, the quiet readiness that marked the beginning of days that might yet demand more than they first appeared to promise.

Below, the inn had already filled.

Merchants spoke in low negotiations over early meals, mercenaries lingered in loose groups, and travelers carried the restless air of those passing through rather than settling in. [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] exchanged a few words with the innkeep, **[[Info/Characters/NPCs/Orgnar\|Orgnar]]**, before settling on [[Info/Locations/Kjalgrad/Kjalgrad\|Kjallgrad]] Sweet Pork. He took his meal to a corner table, removing his helmet only once seated, the act less comfort than necessity.

The inn’s noise did not intrude. It layered around him — movement, coin, quiet impatience — the ordinary sounds of a place built on trade.

When the plate was empty, he returned the helmet to its place and stepped back into the cold.

[[Info/Characters/Party/The Horses\|Rimefang]] waited where he always did. The horse shifted at [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]]’s approach, a small acknowledgment rather than excitement, the steady composure of an animal long accustomed to travel and delay alike. [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] checked tack and breath, finding nothing amiss. The routine required little time, yet he did not rush it.

From the stables, the Tollgate Market spread outward in its usual complexity — stalls pressed close, voices rising and falling with bargaining rhythms that felt almost rehearsed. Here, nearly anything could be found, provided the price was accepted without protest.

At one stall, a Nord woman displayed carved wooden figurines shaped after the old totems: beasts rendered with simple lines that favored recognition over ornament. When [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] asked where they were made, she spoke of a nearby smithy where her husband worked, their crafts overlapping in small ways that made the pieces possible.

[[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] selected one in the shape of [[Info/Lore/Tsun\|Tsun]]’s bear — broad-shouldered, the weight of the carving resting in its stance more than its detail. The choice lingered with a quiet familiarity, less memory of a place than of the idea the totem carried: endurance, trial, the promise that some thresholds were meant to be crossed rather than avoided. He kept the figurine without ceremony and continued through the market.

It was there that a man approached who claimed recognition. The introduction resolved into familiarity: [[Info/Characters/NPCs/Hedrek_Black-Foot\|Hedrek Black-Foot]], representing interests that had once been present in the city and now lingered only in absence. He led [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] to a counting-room above a warehouse near [[Info/Locations/Kjalgrad/Kjalgrad\|Kjallgrad]]’s outer market, where [[Info/Characters/NPCs/Silla_Fenmark\|Silla Fenmark]] waited among ledgers and travel chests prepared for movement rather than permanence.

Their explanation unfolded without urgency but carried weight.

Years earlier, the [[Info/Organizations/Guilds/Guild_of_the_Silverroad\|Guild of the Silver Road]] had forced the [[Info/Organizations/Guilds/Black-Foot_Merchant_Guild\|Black-Foot Merchant Guild]] from the city through tariffs, denied permits, and pressure that left little room for resistance. Since then, the Black-Foots had grown elsewhere — routes expanding across the southern Rift and into Eastmarch — and with that growth came the means to return.

The concern was not merely competition. [[Info/Locations/Kjalgrad/Kjalgrad\|Kjallgrad]], they believed, was shifting in ways that trade alone could not explain: coin moving too quickly, new mint appearing without clear origin, fortunes rising with unnatural speed. Prosperity that lacked patience often concealed its cost.

[[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]]’s presence, they said, carried a usefulness that did not rely on status. He was noticed without being studied, accepted without being trusted — a position that allowed observation to pass as coincidence. With the group’s ongoing inquiries in the region, the overlap felt inevitable.

He agreed without negotiation. The request aligned too closely with questions already forming.

Back in the market, waiting for Hedrek and Silla to prepare, [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]]’s attention settled on a figure that did not belong to any single pattern. An Imperial noble moved between stalls purchasing items that shared no obvious purpose: clothing, baked goods, trinkets chosen without hesitation and abandoned just as quickly. The behavior suggested intention without clarity.

[[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] followed at a distance, observation rather than pursuit. The trail led toward a tavern, yet before he could enter, Hedrek and Silla intercepted him, the timing practical rather than suspicious, and redirected his attention to the meeting ahead.

Beyond the city walls stood the Grand Guildhouse of the Silver Road — stone carved with reliefs of wagons, scales, and ships that spoke of permanence as much as success. After inspection by guards, they were ushered inside to a chamber prepared for negotiation.

Two men awaited them. [[Info/Characters/NPCs/Jorven_Halliksson\|Jorven Halliksson]], guildmaster, carried the composure of someone accustomed to outcomes favoring him. Beside him stood [[Info/Characters/NPCs/Vaelin_Marr\|Vaelin Marr]], whose stillness suggested a different authority, one less visible yet more deliberate.

Discussions moved through tariffs, inspections, supervision of caravans — the language of compromise shaped to limit it. [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] watched rather than listened, allowing details to gather at the edges: guards rotating too frequently to be routine, coin samples that carried a faint trace of brimstone, scribes whose attention drifted toward Vaelin even when Jorven spoke.

When the scent was questioned, the explanation arrived easily — a new minting process, minor complications yet to be resolved. The answer satisfied the room without dispelling the observation.

During recess, they were led to an inner gallery overlooking the vaults. Below, the muted clink of coin carried upward through stone, a steady rhythm that suggested scale more than wealth. Footsteps and murmured exchanges blurred into background continuity.

Vaelin approached [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] there.

The conversation moved in suggestion rather than statement. Vaelin spoke of perspective, of the difficulty in separating profit from consequence, of opportunities that favored those who recognized change before it declared itself. An offer followed — employment as personal guard — framed as practicality.

[[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]]’s acceptance was conditional. Protection for the Black-Foots, favorable entry, stability within whatever shift Vaelin implied. The refusal was immediate but measured, the price described as premature rather than unreasonable.

A second proposal replaced it. An investigation of **[[Info/Characters/NPCs/The_Vestige\|Sadrith Indoril]]**, a Dunmer said to be troubling merchants through questions that exceeded courtesy. Vaelin’s reasoning remained indistinct, answers deflected rather than denied. [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] agreed only to speak and report.

When negotiations resumed, the distance between positions proved unchanged. The Black-Foots declined the Silver Road’s demands, and the meeting concluded without resolution, formality masking tension that remained intact.

The return to the city carried a quieter weight. At the **Silver Antler**, they paused for food and drink, the stop serving less as celebration than recalibration. From there, [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] turned toward the abandoned guildhouse that had once belonged to the Black-Foots.

[[Info/Characters/NPCs/The_Vestige\|Sadrith Indoril]] was waiting.

Recognition came not through introduction but through implication. The investigator was more than his role suggested — the one known across Tamriel as **[[Info/Characters/NPCs/The_Vestige\|the Vestige]]**, whose presence marked events that rarely remained small. [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] learned that his [[Info/Organizations/Military/Companions\|companions]] had already crossed paths with him and departed to assist elsewhere, leaving the encounter to unfold in quieter terms.

Their work led beneath the city into the sewers, where signs of interference had gathered into confirmation. A Daedric summoning, incomplete yet active, fractured the stillness into movement. The confrontation was brief but uneven: [[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] held one Daedroth in place while another fled. His thrown axe struck poorly, deflecting and landing instead on a skeever that had wandered too near — an outcome incidental yet final.

The remaining Daedroth escaped into darkness. [[Info/Characters/NPCs/The_Vestige\|The Vestige]] pursued without hesitation.

[[Info/Characters/Party/Durov\|Durov]] did not follow. The task had ended where his part concluded.

By the time he returned to the inn, the city’s rhythm had resumed its familiar shape — trade continuing, coin circulating, conversations carrying the illusion that nothing beneath them had shifted. Yet the day had left accumulations rather than answers: the scent of minted gold, offers framed as inevitabilities, and the growing sense that [[Info/Locations/Kjalgrad/Kjalgrad\|Kjallgrad]]’s prosperity was being guided by hands that preferred distance to visibility.

The figurine remained where he had placed it among his belongings — small, deliberate, and uncomplicated in a way little else was.

Such objects endured not because they mattered greatly, but because they did not change.
