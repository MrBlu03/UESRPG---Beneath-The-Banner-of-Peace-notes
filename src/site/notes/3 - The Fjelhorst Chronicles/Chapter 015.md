## Chapter 15 - The Favor & The Hunt

**4th of Sun's Dusk, 2E 600**

The morning began under a pale, half-hearted sun, its light weak against the frost that clung to the windows of [[Info/Locations/Nilan's_House\|Nilan]]'s old house. The Fangs of Fjelhorst stirred to life—quietly, at first. [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Periwyn\|Periwyn]] was the first to rise, her eyes drawn to the basement door below. [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Onasha\|Onasha]] had noticed the night before that Periwyn's letter, the one she had written in grief and exhaustion, still lay upon the table down there. When she mentioned it, her words carried concern, though they came out sharper than she intended. Periwyn took them as intrusion. What began as concern soon turned to confrontation. Onasha warned that guilt could cloud the judgment of even the finest archer. Periwyn, voice trembling though firm, replied that her skill and her pain were not Onasha's to measure. Then, with a swirl of her cloak, she left—to walk, to think, and to breathe without the weight of eyes upon her.

[[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Durov\|Durov]] returned later that afternoon, frost gathering in his beard and weariness in his step. He found Onasha and [[Info/Characters/001 The Fangs of Fjelhorst/Kirya\|Kirya]] by the hearth and greeted them with a short nod. Onasha asked where he had been, her tone somewhere between command and concern. Durov, as always, deflected. But the weight of her stare—and perhaps a bit of his own conscience—loosened his tongue. He spoke of the creature he had seen in the woods: the [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Skinstealer\|Skinstealer]]. He tried to draw it but failed, only managing to copy a rune carved into the bark of a pine. He spoke of a Dunmer caravan, which made Onasha stiffen at the mention, and of a Colovian named Gaius who had drugged him with Nirnroot before vanishing. All he had to show for it were a pendant, a Legion tag marked with the number zero, and unanswered questions. He spoke plainly, ate heartily, and then sat with the Fangs until the fire burned low.

---

**5th of Sun's Dusk**

Periwyn woke first to silence and study. A new passage had revealed itself in _On the Custody of the Dead_, and she traced each line as though afraid it might vanish again. Kirya tended to the hearth. Durov broke his fast on salted rations. Onasha had already gone to tend to Mossback, leading the old horse through the morning fog that rolled low over the roofs of [[Info/Locations/Falderstad\|Falderstad]].

When Durov stepped outside to stretch his legs, an arrow greeted him—quivering in the doorframe, a note tied to its shaft. The message bore [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Stryder\|Stryder]]'s mark. The favor once owed was now to be repaid. He asked Durov, and one companion suited for close combat, to meet him at the old tannery guildhouse on the northeastern edge of town. Durov pocketed the note and went to find Periwyn.

At the same hour, Onasha crossed paths with a hunting party gathering by the gate: an elder named [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Svala\|Svala]], her apprentice [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Hoki\|Hoki]], and a grey-furred hound called Brovin. They sought Kyne's Sacred Prize—the White Stag that had haunted the northern woods for generations. Onasha, intrigued, fetched Kirya. Thus the Fangs split for the day: Durov and Periwyn toward the tannery, and Onasha and Kirya toward the wild.

---

Before long, their paths diverged—their shared morning splintering into two separate hunts. Each Fang followed their calling: one into the cold craft of favors owed, the others into the ancient chase of fang and hoof.

### The Hunt

The morning broke in ribbons of rose and gold as Onasha and Kirya joined Svala and Hoki. Frost crackled beneath their boots, and the forest greeted them in stillness. Kirya asked of the rune Durov had seen, and Svala frowned, saying it resembled Nordic carvings—but wrong, distorted, as if the words themselves resisted meaning. Onasha pressed further, asking after the Skinstealer, but Svala could offer little more than rumor: hides flayed with surgical precision, runes etched into bark, and a creature mocking Kyne's own hunts.

They followed the trail north. The woods began in warmth and light, pine-scented and alive with the hum of morning wind. But the deeper they went, the more the forest darkened. Light turned to amber, then to smoke. There, among crooked trees, they found the first signs: deep, erratic tracks, blood frozen in thin lines across snow, and the scrape of antlers high on a trunk. The stag was running—bleeding.

As they pressed on, the forest changed again. The air thickened; silence grew heavy. Totems began to appear—faces carved into trunks, eyeless, mouths stretched in silent screams. Some were painted in blood, others hung with strips of hide. Brovin, the hound, stopped cold. Svala whispered that this was no longer Kyne's forest. Her breath shook as she spoke.

But Hoki, young and proud, pressed forward. He called the others cowards and ran toward a flicker of white between the trees. Onasha and Kirya tried to stop him—with a thrown snowball and some quick darts—but to no avail. They followed as best they could. What they found was not the proud stag of Kyne's blessing, but a dying creature: white coat streaked with red, sides torn open by five perfect slashes. It struggled for breath, then fell silent in the snow. Its blood had spilled in spirals, deliberate and cruel.

Hoki fell to his knees. The silence that followed was total. Even the wind fled the trees. Then came the howl—low, drawn, neither wolf nor man. It circled them, unseen. Svala raised her bow but her hands trembled. Another howl answered, closer. The trees themselves seemed to lean inward. They ran. The torches sputtered, their breaths loud in their ears, the howls following like laughter from the dark.

At last, they broke from the treeline and saw the fires of Falderstad ahead. Only when their boots struck the frozen road did they stop. Hoki was pale and shaking, unable to speak. Onasha, in rare compassion, drew him close. Svala spat into the snow, declaring that this was no beast of Kyne. Whatever had come to these woods wore her blessings as a mask. She promised to rally hunters at dawn—the tolling of the Hunter's Bell would mark their stand. The Fangs nodded. The hunt was no longer for Kyne's favor. It was for survival.

They went to [[Info/Locations/The_Soggy_Beaver\|the Soggy Beaver]] that night, their spirits shaken but their resolve firm.

---

### The Favor

While the others hunted, Durov and Periwyn made for the old tannery guildhouse. It stood half-collapsed, its windows boarded, its roof heavy with snow. Inside, the air smelled of old hides and cold stone. A narrow stair led them to a basement lit by lanternlight, where [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Stryder\|Stryder]] awaited. He placed a coin upon the table—silver, marked with a triple ouroboros: a dragon devouring an eagle devouring a lion devouring the dragon. He explained their task. A caravan of Dunmer merchants flew [[Info/Organizations/House_Hlaalu\|Hlaalu]] colors, but their loyalties were suspect. They bought guards, they bought silence, and they would be passing through Falderstad by noon. The Fangs were to intercept them under pretense of royal inspection, uncover their cargo, and bring back anything suspicious. Stryder's men would be hidden in the trees, should things sour.

They left at once. The Treva River ran beside them, half-frozen beneath the winter sun. Before long, they saw the wagons: two of them, flanked by six Dunmer guards and a Nord driver. Durov presented an official writ, and Periwyn announced the inspection in a voice of command, invoking the full name and titles of [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Queen_Freidr\|Queen Freidr]] the Patient—Jarl of Riften, Matriarch of Clan Volng, Shield of the Eastmarch Border, Bear-Mother of the Rift, Keeper of the Watchfires, and Blood-Daughter of Ysgramor. The guards hesitated but complied.

At first glance, everything seemed proper—cloth, mead, fish, spice. Yet Durov's instincts clawed at him. He questioned one of the Dunmer, whose fear betrayed him more than his words. His lies came quick and sloppy, each one contradicting the last. Durov leaned in, feigning belief, prodding him further with dry amusement—when the Dunmer presented a document in Dunmeri, Durov noted the missing seal and asked, "Do they not have wax where you're from?" The man stammered, inventing excuses that only worsened his case. At last, cornered and trembling, he admitted to a hidden compartment in the second wagon. Beneath its boards they found what they sought: vials of moon sugar and a ledger marked in both Hlaalu script and the ouroboros seal.

When Durov demanded translation, one of the guards shouted something in Dunmeri. Periwyn already had him in a headlock, demanding answers, but when the others simply stared and one coldly said, "Do it," she hesitated only a heartbeat before snapping the man's neck. The rest stood frozen in shock. Durov continued his inspection, planting a single vial of moon sugar beneath the boards of the first wagon as insurance. When questioned by the city guard upon return, the evidence was irrefutable.

Helga Cairn-Shield herself met them at the gate. She did not mourn for Dunmer smugglers, only promised that the Jarl would confirm the executions in due time. She directed them back to the tannery—Stryder wished to see the evidence firsthand.

In the basement, they found him again—this time with another figure, hooded, voice cold. When Durov demanded a name, the stranger replied only, "No one of consequence." Durov didn't buy it; he stepped forward, blocking the way to the stairs. Before the air could thicken, Periwyn spoke, sharp and calm, telling Durov to stand down. The hooded figure chuckled, mocking, and said, "Good girl." The words made her freeze. Stryder thanked them for the work, said that their business was concluded, and hinted that there might be paid work in the future. Periwyn turned first to leave, and Durov followed behind, eyes fixed on the stairs rather than the shadow they left behind.

---

### Together Again

That night, the Fangs reunited in [[Info/Locations/The_Soggy_Beaver\|the Soggy Beaver]]. The tavern was alive with noise, but their table sat under a dim lamp, the air thick with stories. Onasha vented her frustration over Hoki's recklessness. Kirya and Periwyn listened in silence, while Durov drank, content to let others speak. Before the night's end, they convinced [[Info/Characters/999 NPC's/Bjalfi\|Bjalfi]] the innkeeper to spread word through town—that hunters and warriors alike were needed at dawn, to face the Skin-Stealer in its lair.

When the mugs ran dry, they returned to Nilan's house. The hearth was warm, the night cold. One by one, they drifted to sleep—Fangs of Fjelhorst, united again beneath a single roof, as the wind outside howled like something calling their names. It was the quiet calm before the next storm, and none of them yet knew how swiftly it would break.
