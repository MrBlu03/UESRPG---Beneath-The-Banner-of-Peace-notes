## Chapter 33 – Hospitality of Coin

**Middas, 17th of Evening Star, 2E 600**

The day began blanketed in snow.

It seemed, at last, that the two weeks of clear sun had finally run their course — and winter, which had been circling the Rift like a patient wolf, had closed in for good.

In the Tomb of the Oath-Breaker King, Onasha awoke with stiff limbs and the particular ache of someone who had slept deeply but not well. Periwyn was already awake, though still in a poor humor despite days of rest — a mood that seemed, lately, to have taken up residence rather than passed through. Not wishing to disturb her further, Onasha stretched quickly and made her way to Mossback, having missed her equine companion dearly over the last few days.

Durov woke shortly after. He armored himself as was his habit, took a carrot from the provisions, and walked out to Rhymefang. There, he thanked Onasha for the anvil she had gifted him the day before — which she explained had come from a Camonna Tong camp, on the reasonable grounds that corpses would have no need of it.

And it was then that Durov had the most wonderful of ideas.

Where there is an anvil, there is likely a forge. And since the anvil had come from a camp rather than any permanent structure, the forge — if there was one — would surely be movable. And movable things, in Durov's experience, were merely heavy, never impossible.

So the two of them took the makeshift sled Onasha had used — a table with its legs cut off, unceremoniously conscripted into sled service — and set off through the fresh snow.

---

Kjallgrad, which they passed along the south, was quieter than Onasha remembered.

Durov had noticed it himself over the last few days, which he had spent working with Jalf at *The Maker's Forge*. The violence and the protests had been slowly dying down since the Jarl's Hall burned. But this morning there was something new.

The eastern gate was closed. And along the walls stood guards — perhaps three or four dozen of them, their eyes fixed on the distance.

Onasha spotted the reason first: columns of smoke, rising far out along the eastern road. Three of them. Someone was camped out there, waiting. Preparing.

The forge, they decided, could wait. Unlike the situation before the gate, it had little chance of running away or escalating.

So they slid their way east, toward the campfires.

Durov, naturally, immediately concluded it was a siege, and waxed eloquent about how three little campfires would not be nearly enough to besiege a city — what a waste of the guards' time.

By Akatosh, was he wrong.

Not about the waste of time. About the siege.

For it quickly became apparent that this was no siege party at all, but a sizable caravan of the Black-Foot Company, blue-and-tan colors flying everywhere about the camp. Having worked with them before — in Kjallgrad, and before that in Riften — Durov was swiftly recognized by the guards, and led directly to a tent where two familiar faces sat waiting.

Hedrek, a senior official of the Company. And Silla Fenmark, his ledger mistress. Durov had met them both weeks earlier, just before taking the job from Lord Marr. Even then, the Black-Foot Company had been trying to regain its old foothold in the city — though the negotiations with the Silver Road had ended going nowhere.

Hedrek, ever the gracious host, greeted them and told them to sit.

It turned out the Black-Foot Company had been looking for the Fangs already — so perhaps it was fate that brought them here at this hour. The Company, you see, was here to stay. Officially. With permission.

Silla laid out the proof: a document bearing the seal of the Queen of Riften — an official charter granting the Black-Foot Company the right to take over Silver Road holdings, personnel included, and to maintain stability in Kjallgrad until a new Jarl was chosen.

But there was a problem.

While Clan Black-Foot was largely respected in the Hold, the Silver Road had renounced all fealty to the crown — and a charter, however official, cannot open gates. The guards had been ordered to keep the Company out at all costs.

*But they themselves had not been paid.*

And that was what Hedrek and the Black-Foot Company wished to exploit. They would hire the guards en masse — fair wages, hot meals, lodging — but only until the next Jarl was chosen. The guards, however, refused to budge without proof that the Company had their best interests at heart. The Company was liquid, but not bottomless. The priority, of course, was to pay the guards' debts with whatever coin remained in the Silver Road's own vaults first, and only then reach into Company pockets.

Silla, as ever, had a plan to kill two birds with one stone.

While the Company could not approach the walls, nobody could stop coin reaching families — not if you knew how to get it inside. Durov and Onasha would deliver the gold to a few families whose voices mattered most, with written receipts naming the terms of service. The difficulty was that one cannot pay exact wages owed without proof — and proof meant the paymaster's ledger.

The paymaster, however, had disappeared a week prior.

But his ledger, Silla had learned from *reliable sources*, remained at his home — where his wife still lived. In exchange for the ledger, the Company agreed to pay her full arrears on the spot, along with protection and a clerk's pension. The pair would visit her first, then the two other families on the list.

As for how to get the coin into the city — Durov had a plan of his own.

He had worked with the Guild for some time, or rather with its Coiled Path liaison, and was known and trusted among the guards. He suggested taking their cart, still sitting at the Old Tomb, loading it with gold, and simply driving it in under the guise of smithy work.

And, thanks to their early arrival, there was plenty of time. Onasha would ride to the old tomb alone — the horse could not carry two, and returning with two horses would be cumbersome for someone untrained — collect the cart and Rhymefang, and bring the gold back.

Durov, in the meantime, decided to have some fun at the camp.

He wandered for a while, taking in the warmth of the fires, chatting with familiar faces — until he spotted a few children running about in the snow.

Like a self-serving advisor whispering in a king's ear, he began dropping subtle suggestions among them, poisoning each against the other. Then — with the swiftness of a mule — one of them was struck. And then another. Snowballs flew across the yard, covering faces with untold amounts of snow.

It did not take long for Durov himself to be hit, squarely across the back of the head.

He turned slowly, his expression — joy, most likely — hidden behind the plate of his helm. He regarded the children for a few long seconds. Then, with speed and grace wholly unnatural for a man of his stature, he grabbed a handful of snow and threw it directly at one of them.

What ensued was a snowball fight so vicious that the Company's own guards joined in, calling it war practice. Before long, nearly half the camp was involved, hurling snow across the entire area.

---

Onasha arrived at the old tomb less than an hour later.

While she readied Rhymefang, she caught a glimpse of the Vestige, still walking the halls, and contemplated whether to ask him along. Before she could settle the question with herself, the Vestige arrived at the horses and asked what the tone of Kjallgrad would be today.

Onasha replied hastily that the Black-Foot Company — with which he was tangentially familiar — was camped outside the eastern gate with a charter, and that she was going to help.

He agreed to come.

He had to finally make himself useful, she said.

By the time they were mounted, the Vestige had already procured new garments — a leather and fur affair, suitable to the weather and to anonymity — and told her to refer to him as one *Danel Sarano*, a mercenary.

On the ride over, the subject of Kjallgrad's fate came up quickly.

Onasha's concerns were, as ever, with the people. How long until the next Jarl took power? What would become of the city's current structure? Why could Queen Freidr simply step in, as other monarchs and governors had done across the continent?

All simple questions — with a more complicated answer.

Skyrim, you see, was not a land of feudal monarchs and lawful writ. It was a land of clan holdings and pledged fealty. Kjallgrad and its lands belonged to Clan Morvan — and Queen Freidr, whatever respect she wielded, had no lawful, written ownership over that land. All she could offer were what were, in effect, letters of support from the Kingdom of the Rift.

Now, the Queen's clan — Clan Volng — commanded a great deal of such respect. Her father had presided over the Rift before her, and though she had been young when she took the throne, she had managed to keep peace among the cities long enough for them to pledge to her. But Clan Morvan, with all its heirs and associates dead, would in truth be lost — its holdings, the city itself, its charter, all of it — up for grabs by another clan.

In this case, it seemed, Clan Black-Foot was the likeliest candidate.

As for the people? They would simply carry on, as Nords always had — adapting to the next Jarl.

Or burning his corpse on a pyre.

Then Danel made his introduction.

He announced himself as a mercenary — and Durov, quick as ever, found a use for the fiction. Danel would play his client, a man in need of a weapon repaired. Danel, not wishing to put his own expensive weapons at risk, would simply buy a replacement from the Black-Foot Company. A flawless arrangement, on paper.

Durov then proceeded to snap the sword at the hilt.

They climbed onto the cart and left for the city.

---

By the time they reached the eastern gate, the snowfall had worsened, and visibility had dropped drastically.

With Onasha and Danel on the back of the cart, alongside several crates of *ores and metals*, Durov was stopped by the guards just in front of the gate. From above, a man shouted questions about their business in town — then came down to speak face to face.

With Irlav gone south with Lord Marr, Durov judged this to be the next highest-ranking officer, a senior man who had taken command in his absence. It turned out that what kept the guards upon the wall was not spite, but something older: loyalty. Many years of service to a guild do not simply fall away. The officer, who until the week before had been one of Lord Marr's own men, was taking his orders from the guildmaster himself — Jorven Halliksson, who, he said, Durov should seek out regarding any changes.

As they say: a Nord rarely breaks his oath.

Their first stop was *The Maker's Forge*, where Durov repaired Danel's sword. Danel paid the master smith, Jalf, and then set off to see what, perhaps, the Wharf Rats might have need of.

Durov and Onasha proceeded to their first delivery — the paymaster's house.

The house sat quietly in a corner of what the locals called Weaver's Row, not far from the Black-Foot Company's old guildhouse. When they knocked, the door opened a hand's width, and Gretar, the paymaster's wife, greeted them with a face older than her years.

Durov introduced himself and his companion as men wishing to speak about her and her husband's arrears. After some brief pleasantries, he laid out the terms while Onasha recorded the conversation on paper — if only out of respect for the second party.

As Silla had promised: full arrears paid, protection, and a pension.

Gretar was hesitant at first. Then she agreed, and went to fetch the books.

What she handed over was a ledger, perhaps as thick as her hand, filled with entries. The most recent ones were there — the older archive still sat inside the Silver Road's vaults. Durov and Onasha flipped through the pages, and found several strange entries among them.

Some caught Durov's eye in particular.

A neat column of entries, each marked with a three-headed ouroboros — not the Guild's own sigil. And beside them, a debt to *Clan Stone-Singer*.

That name made Durov pause. Recognition flickered briefly in his mind — a memory of training his shield stance with someone he could not place. A name from his past, whose details still eluded him.

And the last entries were the most damning of all.

Lord Marr had made several large withdrawals from the Guild's coffers.

It was as they had feared.

The vault was empty.

Durov thanked Gretar for her hospitality, wished her well, and hoped aloud that things would return to stability soon.

---

The next house on the list belonged to a man named Beyrik, a few streets from the eastern gate — somewhat larger than its neighbors, and neat.

A young woman answered the door with a knife in her hand.

After the guard captain's departure, Beyrik had remained the most senior officer in the Guild, and it had fallen to him to look after his men. For the past two weeks he had been paying some of the men's arrears from his own pocket — which had left him struggling, and sown bad blood between him and those he could not pay.

His wife, Maren, accepted the coin and terms graciously — though she made it clear she did not wish for charity. She simply wanted her husband home. She wrote a quick letter with the parchment and quill they offered, and asked them to deliver it to her husband the next time they saw him.

As they were leaving, Maren told them one more thing.

Beyrik might have kept the guards from starving so far. But if his money ran out — he would fight. Simply because he would have nothing left to lose.

---

The last house was small, tucked away in the Old Quarter, not far from the smithy.

The door opened on its own at a single knock.

Inside, in a small warm room, sat an elderly woman at a spinning wheel, with a wall of family names carved into the lintel above her. She greeted them with the hospitality of a doting grandmother, setting them near the fire.

Her son, Ranveig, she said, would be the hardest of the three to convince. Proud. Angry. Fiercely loyal. His one weakness — his one crack in the armor — was simply her.

She accepted the terms.

And when Durov asked whether she wanted the door fixed, as it was not closing properly, and she mentioned that she'd been meaning to ask Jalf, he immediately chimed in — a coworker of the man's, he'd be happy to do it for free.

Hearing that, she accepted the help warmly.

---

They left Ranveig's house and pointed the cart toward the Silver Road guildhouse — Durov wished to speak with Guildmaster Jorven himself.

Somewhere along the way, Onasha spotted someone following them.

Durov, without ceremony, hopped off the cart as they rounded a corner, and bumped directly into the man — who turned out to be decently small and scruffy, and who tried to lie his way out of the collision by claiming he needed to reach the inn.

Durov pointed out that the inn lay in entirely the opposite direction.

The man tried again. Durov leaned in — and it took only a moment of that particular Colovian intimidation for the fellow to confess the truth: he was a messenger for the guards, tasked with keeping track of what they were doing in the city.

Durov, unbothered, informed him that he would be riding along with them.

Since that, after all, was where they were headed anyway.

They arrived at the Silver Road guildhouse to find a guard standing at the door — a face familiar to Durov — and the afternoon's true work about to begin.
