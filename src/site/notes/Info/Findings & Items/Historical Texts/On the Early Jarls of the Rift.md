# On the Early Jarls of the Rift

_A damaged chronicle attributed to Brother Vethran of [[Info/Locations/The Rift/Riften\|Riften]]
Likely composed in the late First Era_

---

## Preface of the Scribe

The valley now known as the Rift has not always been governed by the jarldoms that rule it today.

In the centuries following the fall of the great northern kings, the lands between the Jerall Mountains and the eastern forests were divided among many lesser lords. These jarls ruled valleys, ridges, and river crossings as independent realms, bound together only loosely by shared blood and the old laws of the Nords.

Their halls have long since fallen, their banners lost, and their names remembered only in fragments of song.

This work attempts to gather what remains of those early rulers. Much has already vanished, and what survives comes to us through skalds, weathered cairn-stones, and the occasional passaged preserved in older chronicles.

I do not claim perfect accuracy.

I claim only that these stories once belonged to the Rift, and that forgetting them entirely would be a greater error than recording them incorrectly.

---

## Of the First Valley Jarls

The earliest jarls of the Rift did not rule a single hold but rather a scattering of territories divided by river and forest.

Among the first named in surviving fragments are:

- Jarl Torald fo the Southern Pines, whose hall stood near the ancient road to Cyrodiil
- Jarl Brynja Ice-Eye, remembered in several hunting songs as the first ruler to claim tribute from the fishermen of [[Info/Locations/The Rift/Lake_Honrich\|Lake Honrich]]
- Jarl Hadring, whose tragic fate beside the river is still remembered in the sagas concerning [[Info/Locations/The Rift/Widows_River\|Widow's River]]

These jarls governed not through written law but through strength of arms and loyalty of clan. Alliances were sworn frequently, and broken almost as often.

Yet in those turbulent years there remained certain sacred traditions. Among these were the oaths sworn before the gods, particularly those taken beneath the open sky in the name of [[Info/Lore/Kyne\|Kyne]].

To break such an oath was considered among the gravest of crimes.

It is therefore curious that the most infamous ruler of the early Rift became known precisely for that offense.

---

## The King Without Honor

Among the many half-forgotten rulers of the Rift there exists one figure whose memory appears to have been deliberately obscured.

Some sagas name him Jorund the Twice-Sworn.
Others refer to him only as the King Without Honor.

Later skalds often avoid naming him altogether, calling him simply the Oath-Breaker.

What little can be gathered suggests he rose to power during a time when the western valleys of the Rift were divided among several rival jarls. Through a mixture of cunning diplomacy and ruthless opportunism, Jorund gradually brought these lands beneath his influence.

Unlike other warlords of the time, he preferred oaths to battles.

He traveled from hall to hall swearing loyalty to neighboring jarls, binding himself to them through sacred rites before the gods. These alliances were often sealed with feasts, gifts of iron, and solemn vows spoken beneath the open sky.

Yet each oath he swore he later betrayed.

One by one he turned upon those who had trusted him, attacking their halls when their warriors were absent or their defenses weak. By the time the remaining jarls understood his pattern, much of the western Rift already lay beneath his control.

His hall was built upon a ridge overlooking a bend of the valley river, in a place where the foothills rise toward the mountains.

For a time he ruled there as a petty king.

But the Nords are a people who remember insults.

Eventually three mountain jarls united their clans against him. These lords ruled the high valleys beyond the western forests, and their warriors were said to be among the fiercest in the region.

Their names appear in several sagas as Skorvald, Hjorn, and Valdyr.

Later skalds would remember them together as the Three Stone Brothers.

---

## The War of the Western Ridge

When the mountain jarls marched against the Oath-Breaker, they did not come quietly.

Their warbands descended from the high ridges like an avalanche of iron and shields. The king’s forces met them in the foothills near the river valley, where narrow ridges and broken stone made the fighting savage and confused.

The chronicles describe a great slaughter on both sides.

The king’s hall was burned.
His warriors were scattered.
And the mountain jarls themselves suffered grievous losses before the battle ended.

Yet the most curious detail recorded in every surviving account is this:

The Oath-Breaker was not slain in battle.

He was taken alive.

---

## Judgement Beneath the Mountain

The jarls who defeated him argued long into the night regarding his fate.

Among the Nords, oath-breaking is punishable by death. Yet the sagas claim the jarls feared that executing him openly would stain their own honor, for they had once sworn friendship with the man before he betrayed them.

To kill him as a common criminal would make them oath-breakers as well.

Thus they chose another punishment.

They ordered that a stone chamber be built beneath the ridge where the battle had taken place. Within this hall they sealed the defeated king, entombing him alive beneath the mountain he had once ruled.

The skalds say the jarls then swore a final oath: that the king’s name would be erased from their songs so that no man of Skyrim would remember him with honor.

Only his shame would remain.

A fragment preserved in a damaged manuscript reads:

> “Let him lie where oath and earth bind fast,
> And let no son of Skyrim speak his name.
> For the dead remember promises broken,
> And the stone remembers shame.”

---

## The Stones of the Brothers

In the years that followed, three tall pillars of stone were raised upon the ridge where the battle had been fought.

Whether these were grave markers for the fallen jarls or monuments to their oath is uncertain.

Over time the people of the Rift began calling them the Three Stone Brothers.

Later sagas would claim that [[Info/Lore/Kyne\|Kyne]] herself turned the jarls to stone so their vow would endure forever. Others insist the stones merely mark the place where the jarls swore never again to break faith with one another.

What became of the tomb beneath the ridge is unknown.

Some hunters claim the earth there occasionally trembles on stormy nights. Others insist the wind between the stones sounds like distant war-horns.

Such tales are, of course, difficult to confirm.

---

## Closing Words

The early jarls of the Rift ruled in violent and uncertain times.

Their halls have vanished.  
Their banners are dust.  
Their battles survive only in the half-remembered songs of skalds.

Yet the land itself remembers.

Rivers keep their names.  
Mountains hold their secrets.  
And sometimes, where three stones stand upon a lonely ridge, the past waits quietly beneath the earth.

-- _Brother Vethran of Riften_
