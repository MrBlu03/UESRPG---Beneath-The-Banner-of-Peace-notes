# A Survey of the Eastern Valleys of the Rift

_With Observations on Ancient Foundations and Sites of Possible Antiquity_

Recorded by Halvius Mertian, Imperial Surveyor
In the 184th Year of the Second Era

---

## Prefatory Remarks

While conducting a general survey of the interior lands north of the Treva River, I was instructed not merely to chart rivers, roads, and farmlands, but also to take notes of any signs suggesting earlier habitation.

The holds known as The Rift is littered with the remnants of earlier ages, collapsed halls, burial cairns, and foundations of uncertain purpose. Many such sites lie far from the principal roads and are therefore poorly recorded.

This report concerns the eastern valleys surrounding the upper course of the Treva River, particularly a basin known locally as the Silvery Valley. The region is sparsely settled, even by Skyrim's standards, yet contains several geographical features which, in my judgment, would have made it suitable for ancient habitation or ceremonial construction.

---

## The Treva River Corridor

The Treva River flows westwards across the Rift, forming a narrow valley between Lake Honrich in the east and Lake Geir in the west. In the region examined here the river occupies a relatively narrow valley bordered by forested slopes and low ridges.

The eastern bank of the river rises into a series of wooded hills that form a natural boundary between the river corridor and the interior basin beyond. These ridges are of particular interest, as they provide both elevation and visibility across the surrounding terrain, qualities that often influenced the placement of early Nordic structures.

Several sections of these ridges appear to contain worked stone fragments, though erosion and vegetation have obscured their origin.

---

## The Basin of the Silver Valley

East of the Treva lies a sheltered basin referred to by local inhabitants as the Silver Valley. The valley stretches north to south between several natural barriers:

- the plateau of the Altan Mesa to the north,
- a chain of wooded hills and small peaks to the south,
- and the rising Honrich Highlands to the east.

The basin slopes gently westward toward the Treva River and is crossed by several small tributary streams descending from the surrounding heights.

The valley floor is largely grassland interspersed with stands of aspen and birch. Its fertile soil would have supported modest agriculture even in earlier eras, suggesting that scattered habitation may have existed here long before the present settlement.

---

## Kjallgrad and the Central Tributary

Near the center of the valley stands the small village of Kjallgrad, located beside a modest stream that descends from the northern plateau before bending westward toward the Treva.

This tributary forms a shallow valley of its own, bordered by low banks and clusters of older pine growth. The surrounding ground is firm and moderately elevated compared to the nearby floodplain.

During my survey I observed several irregular stone fragments embedded in the soil along the western side of this stream valley. Whether these stones are natural or remnants of earlier construction remains uncertain.

## The Southern Ridge

Between Kjallgrad and the taller peaks stands a narrow wooded ridge rising above the valley floor. The ridge runs roughly north to south and provides an elevated vantage over both the central basin and the river corridor.

The slope facing the valley is gradual and covered in mixed birch forest, while the western face descends more sharply toward the Treva's wooded banks.

This ridge contains several unusual stone formations protruding from the soil. Most appear natural, though a few stand with a regularity that suggest possible shaping by human hands.

A traveler approaching from the valley floor would notice these stones prominently silhouetted along the ridge line.

The ground around them contains scattered rubble and sections of partially buried masonry. Whether these fragments belong to an ancient structure or to later cairn-building cannot presently be determined.

---

## The Altan Mesa Escarpment

North of the valley the land rises steeply into the plateau known as Altan Mesa.

The southern edge of this mesa forms a high escarpment overlooking the basin below. Several narrow ravines descend from the plateau into the valley, carrying seasonal streams during the spring melt.

The escarpment itself offers commanding views across much of the Silver Valley and the Treva corridor beyond. Such positions were historically favored for watch-posts or signal fires.

No standing structures were observed during my ascent, though scattered cairns appear along the upper ridges.

---

## The Honrich Highlands

TO the east the valley rises gradually into the Honrich Highlands, which form the transitional terrain between the Rift basin and the slopes of the Velothi Mountains.

The highlands are rugged and irregular, consisting of forested ridges broken by narrow passes. Several cold streams descend westward, forming small basins and lakes that dot the landscape, though most remain shallow except during the spring thaw.

These uplands would have provided natural concealment for smaller structures or burial sites, particularly in areas where the ground consists of exposed bedrock suitable for carving chambers.

No obvious ruins were located during my limited exploration of this region, though the windy conditions, prominence of predatory fauna, and lack of civilization away from the road make thorough examination difficult.

---

## On Sites Suitable for Ancient Construction

In considering the placement of early Nordic structures within the Silver Valley region, several geographical factors appear significant.

Ancient builders favored locations that combined:

- elevated ground,
- proximity to water,
- and natural defensibility.

Within the surveyed region the southern ridge between Kjallgrad and its southern peaks fulfills all three conditions.

The ridge commands a clear view across the valley while remaining close to the central tributary stream. Its stone outcroppings and partially buried masonry suggest that some form of earlier construction may once have stood there.

Whether this site represents remains of a watch-post, cairn complex, or burial structure cannot be stated with certainty.

---

## Concluding Notes

The eastern valleys of the Rift remain only lightly explored compared to the lands surrounding Lake Honrich and the larger trade roads.

Nevertheless, the geography of the Silver Valley, particularly the aforementioned southern ridge, suggests the region may contain remnants of earlier Nordic activity.

Further excavation or organized study would be required to determine the nature of the stone fragments observed during this survey.

Until such efforts are undertaken, the valley remains an unassuming but intriguing corner of the Eastern Rift, where the landscape hints at foundations older than the present settlements.
