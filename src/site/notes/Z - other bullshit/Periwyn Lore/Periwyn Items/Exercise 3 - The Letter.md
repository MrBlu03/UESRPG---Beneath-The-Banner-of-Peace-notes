# Forgive me

I keep Aerendyl frozen at nine. I draw him at ages he never reached. Celebrate birthdays with a ghost. When I cast lightning, I tell myself he's still beside me. That his hand is on my shoulder. That he whispers "Wynnie" when the spells crackle through my fingers.

But I don't remember his laugh anymore. Only the wet sound of Gareth's blade. Only his death. I've made my brother into a weapon. Turned his memory into justification. Forgive me.

Father taught me merchant ciphers. Fair dealing. Numbers that balanced. I twisted them into the mathematics of murder. His lessons now catalog kills. Mother sang to me. Called me "little flower." Delicate but enduring. I can't remember the songs anymore. Only her snarl. Can't remember her touch. Only the cold. Vengeance tarnished every memory of them. Forgive me.

Gareth found me in grave dirt trying to raise my brother. He saw the hurt child beneath the rage. Showed mercy. And I killed him. I tell myself it was mercy. The death he begged for. But his vision asked what I'd do if he came back seeking a cure. I don't know if I would have helped him. Can't see the person beneath the bite anymore. That's what I've become. Forgive me. Forgive me.

Phaelan warned me I was becoming a monster. He was right. Liselle told me to find her when the hunt was finished, "if anything of me remains." Nothing remains.

Toren died because of my arrogance. I gave his death one page in my journal. One page. Clinical. Factual. Bloodless. Like inventory, not a friend. His blood is on my hands. Please forgive me.

I can't remember Marielle's face. Or Elric's. Or Darna's. Only their tactical value. I categorized them like vampires. Never just sat with them. Never laughed. Vengeance ate those memories.

Beitild and Olen at Greenwall. The hunters whose names I never learned. The families. The thralls. The villages I reached too late. Everyone I used to justify my hate.

Forgive me. All of you. Forgive me.

Seventeen years wasted. Seventeen years of tarnishing their memories by making them fuel for hate.

What would they think of me now? The merchant's daughter who uses her father's lessons to catalog death? The little flower who became a blade? The girl who loved her brother so much she forgot how to love anything but vengeance?

I've pushed away everyone who tried to help. Phaelan. Liselle. Toren. Marielle. Elric. Darna. Even now—Kirya, Onasha, Durov. I keep them at arm's length. Catalog their tactical value. Never let them in. Because if I let them in, I might have to feel. And if I feel, the walls might come down. And if the walls come down, I'll have to face what I've done. Who I've become.

I'm terrified I'm just empty. That I burned everything good away and there's nothing left but ash and screaming and a nine-year-old girl who died in those ruins. Twenty-seven years old. No home. No family. No purpose beyond destruction. Three companions I barely let myself call friends.

They wouldn't recognize me. And that's the worst betrayal of all.

The book says to record what I felt, not what I wished.

I wished to feel Aerendyl's hand. Mother's touch. Father's pride. Gareth's forgiveness.

What I felt: Regret. Crushing, suffocating regret that I've wasted my life. That I've dishonored everyone I loved by making them monuments of hate instead of remembering the joy.

And beneath it—barely there, fragile, terrifying—possibility. That maybe Periwyn Lunclaire isn't entirely dead. That maybe I could learn to remember them with love instead of vengeance.

Maybe.

But I don't know how. The Oath binds. The hunt continues. The vampires remain.

And I'm so tired of being the weapon I forged myself into.

Mortgren wrote this isn't power—it's responsibility. That responsibility is rarer than power.

Maybe my responsibility isn't to hunt until I'm as dead as they are. Maybe it's to remember. To truly remember. Not the deaths. Not the failures. Not the screams. The life before. The love before.

Maybe I owe them that. To become someone they'd recognize. Someone they'd be proud of.

Not a hunter. Not a weapon. Not a monument of grief.

Just Periwyn.

I don't know if I can. But tonight, in this dead man's basement, I stopped. I listened. I wrote the truth.

It's not much.

But maybe it's enough to start.

Forgive me. All of you. Forgive me for what I've become.

And if you can't—if you're too far beyond to hear, too lost in whatever comes after—then maybe I need to learn to forgive myself.

I don't know how to do that either.

But I'll try.
